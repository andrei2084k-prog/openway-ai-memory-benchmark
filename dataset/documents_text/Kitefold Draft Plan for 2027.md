# Kitefold Draft Plan for 2027

Kitefold, Inc. | Port Calloway | Draft dated October 29, 2026 | Prepared by Maya Lindqvist, founder and CEO

Status: draft for discussion with the leadership team. Nothing in this document is approved. Numbers marked as plan are targets, not forecasts we have committed to anyone outside the company.

1. Purpose of this draft

This document sets out where Kitefold stands, what we want to achieve in 2027, who we need to hire, which product bets we want to make, and what could go wrong. I wrote it to give Arjun, Priya, Marcus, Diego and the rest of the leads something concrete to argue with. I would rather hear that a number is wrong in this draft than discover it later. Please mark it up, and I will fold the comments into a second draft before anything is final.

2. Where we are

As of October 28, 2026, Kitefold has MRR of $102,600. That is the baseline for everything below. It comes from a customer base that is spread across all four plans: Kite, Studio, Stage and Arena. It includes agencies, retail and consumer brands, museums and cultural institutions, education customers and event organizers. No single customer dominates the base, which I count as a strength. Our largest accounts are on the Arena plan, and the most numerous are on Studio and Stage.

We have also lost a few accounts along the way, and I want the plan to be honest about that. A small number of former clients cancelled because their budgets or priorities changed, not because of a failure in the product. Even so, every cancellation is a reminder that retention is worth as much as new sales, and the plan treats it that way.

The product is in better shape than it has been at any earlier point. Several things that were long-term worries have been resolved:

- The Canvas editor is our only editor. The Classic editor has been retired, so we maintain one code path for the editing experience instead of two.

- Legacy Marker tracking has been removed, and the projects that used it were moved to Image Anchor automatically. We no longer carry that maintenance burden.

- Facecast is generally available. It tracks up to 3 faces at once, which covers the use cases customers have asked about so far.

- Floorplace occlusion is in beta for Stage and Arena customers, and we are collecting feedback from them.

- Kitefold for Shopify is live, which gives us a presence in a channel where merchants already work.

- All delivery runs on Petrel CDN after the migration led by Omar. We no longer depend on the provider that had a long outage earlier in 2026, and we ended that contract when traffic moved.

The team is also in a different place. Marcus Feld runs sales and Diego Ferreira runs customer success. Calvin Brooks joined as solutions engineer to help prospects through technical questions before they sign. Noor Haddad is building our partnership work. Two people left us during 2026 for good reasons of their own, and we have kept the work moving. The 2027 hiring plan below is partly about making sure no area depends on a single person.

3. Targets for 2027

The headline target is $2.4 million ARR by December 31, 2027. This is a plan, not a commitment. It is roughly twice what our MRR of $102,600 annualizes to, so reaching it requires near-doubling of recurring revenue within the year. That is ambitious. I think it is achievable only if we improve three things together: new business closed per month, expansion inside existing accounts, and retention.

I suggest we break the target into three parts and agree on the split before the second draft.

a) New business. Most of the growth should come from new paying accounts. Marcus will propose a bottom-up pipeline model, by plan and by segment, that shows how many deals we need to reach the target. Wes owns enterprise deals on the Arena plan and Hannah owns agencies and small business, so each of them should be able to say what their segment can realistically deliver.

b) Expansion. Customers on Kite, Studio and Stage who grow their usage should have a clear and easy path to the next plan. Diego and Marcus should agree on how a customer success manager hands an expansion opportunity to sales, and on who owns the conversation.

c) Retention. Every cancellation subtracts from the target twice: once for the revenue lost and once for the sales effort needed to replace it. Diego should set a retention goal for each plan, and we should review it as often as we review new sales.

Supporting targets, to be set in numbers by each owner in the second draft:

- A pipeline coverage ratio that Marcus considers safe for the revenue target.

- A retention goal for each plan, owned by Diego.

- A response and resolution standard for Kitefold Care, owned by Diego with input from Rosa.

- A platform reliability goal, owned by Arjun and Omar.

- A share of new accounts that come through partners and the Shopify channel, owned by Noor.

4. Hiring plan

We plan to hire 6 people in 2027: 2 account executives, 1 customer success manager, 2 engineers and 1 marketer. This is the largest single investment in the plan, so I want each role tied to a specific problem.

Account executives (2). Wes and Hannah cannot cover the volume that the revenue target implies. One of the new hires should work with agencies and small businesses next to Hannah. The other should work on larger deals next to Wes. Marcus decides the exact split once the pipeline model is ready. I would like to look first at candidates who already understand how marketing campaigns get bought, since our best deals come from people who think in campaigns rather than in software features.

Customer success manager (1). Lena Marsh looks after our Arena accounts, and that work is demanding. The new manager would take on the other plans, working with Diego, so that every paying account has a named person responsible for its health. This hire is aimed at retention and expansion, and it supports the targets in section 3.

Engineers (2). Sam Achterberg owns rendering and Jules Moreau works on the editor, and both are carrying more than a two-person area should. One engineer should strengthen the rendering and tracking side, where Floorplace and Facecast need continued work. The other should work on the editor and on integrations, including Kitefold for Shopify. Arjun will run the technical evaluation. I want it to look like real work in our own product, not a puzzle.

Marketer (1). Ellis Grant runs webinars, LinkedIn and trade shows, and he cannot do all of it and also build a content engine. The new marketer should own content and demand generation, with a focus on case material and partner campaigns. Ellis stays responsible for events and for overall marketing priorities.

Sequencing. We will not open all six roles at once. I suggest opening the roles that most directly support the revenue target first: the account executives and the customer success manager. The engineers and the marketer follow as soon as the first group is in motion. Marcus, Diego and Arjun should propose the order. Hiring more slowly than planned is acceptable. Hiring people we are unsure about is not.

Onboarding. Every new hire should have a first-month plan before they start, a named buddy, and a set of early customer conversations or code changes that they own. For the sales and customer success roles, Calvin should provide technical onboarding so they can speak credibly about the product.

5. Product bets

Priya will lead the product roadmap discussion. These are the bets I think deserve the most attention. They are directions to evaluate, not commitments, and I have deliberately left out dates.

Bet 1: Floorplace occlusion from beta to general availability. The beta is limited to Stage and Arena. The question is whether it is stable enough for broader use and whether it justifies a clearer difference between plans. Priya and Sam should define what "ready" means and what feedback from beta customers we still need.

Bet 2: Facecast depth. Facecast is available and handles up to 3 faces at once. We should ask customers and prospects whether that limit is a real obstacle, and what use cases they would pursue if it were different. We should avoid building capability that nobody asks for.

Bet 3: Commerce. Kitefold for Shopify gave us an entry point into merchant workflows. We should learn what merchants actually want next, and decide whether to deepen the integration or add other commerce connections.

Bet 4: Editor speed and ease of use. With Classic gone, Canvas is the only editor, so any friction in it is felt by everyone. Jules and Tamsin should look at the first-hour experience of a new user, because that is where trials are won or lost.

Bet 5: Content supply. Customers often struggle to get good 3D models. Our contract artists, Lena Marshall for hard-surface product models and Ivo Petrov for photogrammetry scans, help, but we should look at whether a repeatable model-production service or a partner arrangement could remove that obstacle for customers.

Bet 6: Reliability and delivery. The move to Petrel CDN gave us a foundation we trust. Omar and Arjun should set monitoring and incident practices that match the larger customers we want to win.

I would like Priya to bring a short list ranked by customer value and engineering cost, and I expect the list to be shorter than this one. We are a small team, and every bet we take on means another we do not.

6. Go-to-market

Direct sales. Marcus owns the sales plan. The new account executives support it, and Calvin helps prospects through technical evaluation. We should write down a standard way of running a trial so that every prospect gets the same experience.

Partners and agencies. Agencies are a large part of our base and a natural way to reach brands. Noor should develop the partnership approach, and Hannah should keep us close to the agencies' day-to-day needs. We should make it easy for an agency to do repeat work with us.

Marketing. Ellis and the new marketer should concentrate on a few channels where we already see results, rather than adding many new ones. Case material from real customers is the strongest thing we have, and we should use it with each customer's permission.

Customer success. Diego should build a simple rhythm of check-ins by plan, with early warning signs for accounts at risk. Lena Marsh and the new customer success manager carry this out in practice, and Rosa and the Kitefold Care desk give them day-to-day signals from customers.

7. Risks

The main risks to the plan, with a first thought on handling each:

- Hiring risk. If hiring is slow, the revenue target is at risk, because it depends on capacity we do not yet have. Handling: start with the roles closest to revenue, and keep the plan flexible about the order of the rest.

- Concentration in a few people. Some areas rely on one person, such as rendering with Sam and enterprise deals with Wes. Handling: the engineer and account executive hires, plus better documentation and shared ownership.

- Churn. If cancellations rise, we spend our effort replacing revenue instead of adding to it. Handling: the customer success hire and a clear retention goal for each plan.

- Product focus. We could spread ourselves across too many bets. Handling: Priya's ranked list, and a firm limit on the number of bets in progress at once.

- Platform reliability. A serious outage damages trust, especially with larger customers. Handling: Omar's and Arjun's monitoring and incident practices, and honest communication with customers when something goes wrong.

- Dependence on key channels. If the Shopify channel or the partner channel disappoints, growth leans harder on direct sales. Handling: track results by channel and adjust early.

- Ambition of the target. $2.4 million ARR by December 31, 2027 may be too high. Handling: agree on milestones along the way so we can see early whether we are on course and revise the plan openly if we are not.
