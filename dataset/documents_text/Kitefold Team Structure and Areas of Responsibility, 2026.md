# Kitefold Team Structure and Areas of Responsibility, 2026

Kitefold, Inc. | Port Calloway | January 19, 2026

Written by Maya Lindqvist, founder and CEO

1. Purpose of this document

This document says who does what at Kitefold: every person on the team, the role each one holds and the areas each one owns. It also covers our two contract 3D artists and our fractional CFO, because they work with us every day even though they are not on the payroll in the same way. If you are not sure who to ask about something, start here. If the answer is not here, ask me and I will fix the document.

We keep this document short on purpose. It describes ownership, not job descriptions. Where two people touch the same topic, the section below says who decides and who helps.

2. Headcount at a glance

As of January 19, 2026, Kitefold has 15 employees and 2 contract 3D artists. The fractional CFO is an outside adviser and is not counted in either number. The 15 employees are organized into five working areas: leadership, sales, customer success and support, engineering, and marketing and design. The two contractors support the product and customer-project work described in section 8.

3. Leadership

Maya Lindqvist, founder and CEO. I own company direction, hiring decisions, key client relationships, fundraising and the relationship with our fractional CFO. Final calls on pricing and plan structure (Kite, Studio, Stage and Arena) sit with me, after input from Priya and Paloma.

Arjun Mehra, co-founder and CTO. Arjun owns the technical direction of the platform, the engineering roadmap and the quality bar for everything we ship. All engineers work under his direction. When a customer or sales question turns on what the platform can or cannot do, Arjun gives the final technical answer.

Priya Kandasamy, Head of Product since February 2024. Priya owns the product roadmap, the priorities between feature requests and the written specifications engineering builds from. She is the person who decides what goes into the editor and what waits. She works closely with Tamsin on design and with Arjun on feasibility.

4. Sales

Paloma Reyes, Head of Sales since June 2023. Paloma owns the sales process end to end: pipeline, quotas, forecasting, quotes and order forms. Both account executives report to her. She also decides how a lead is routed between the enterprise and the agency and small-business tracks.

Wes Okafor, Account Executive for enterprise (Arena) deals. Wes owns the full cycle for Arena prospects, from first call through contract. He coordinates with Lena Marsh so that an enterprise client is handed over cleanly after signing.

Hannah Brook, Account Executive for agencies and small business. Hannah owns agency and small-business deals, which tend to be shorter and more frequent. She hands signed accounts to Diego Ferreira or, where an agency relationship needs more attention, brings Paloma in.

Routing rule: if a prospect could plausibly sit in either track, Paloma decides within one business day. Wes and Hannah do not trade leads between themselves without her say.

5. Customer success and support

Lena Marsh, Customer Success Manager for Arena accounts. Lena owns onboarding, adoption and renewals for our Arena clients. She is the named contact for those accounts and runs the regular check-ins with them. She raises expansion opportunities with Wes and escalates product problems to Priya.

Diego Ferreira, Customer Success for small-business accounts. Diego owns onboarding and ongoing success for small-business accounts. His work is largely about helping customers get their first experience live and keep it running well. He passes recurring questions to Rosa so that they become better help articles.

Rosa Imbert, support specialist on the Kitefold Care desk. Rosa owns the Kitefold Care queue: she answers incoming questions, diagnoses problems and passes bugs to engineering with a clear description of how to reproduce them. When a question belongs to a specific account, she loops in Lena or Diego rather than answering around them.

A note on names: Lena Marsh (Customer Success) and Lena Marshall (contract 3D artist) are two different people. In messages and tickets, please use the full last name to avoid mix-ups.

6. Engineering

Sam Achterberg, senior engineer for rendering. Sam built the Canvas 3D viewport and remains its owner. Anything about how models look on screen, performance of the viewport or rendering behavior goes to Sam first.

Jules Moreau, frontend engineer on the editor. Jules owns the editor interface that customers use to build experiences. Interface bugs, usability fixes and editor behavior in the browser are his area. He works from Priya's specifications and Tamsin's designs.

Kenji Watanabe, tracking engineer. Kenji owns the tracking work that keeps content anchored in the camera view. Questions about how reliably an experience stays in place, and what conditions affect it, go to him.

Omar Siddiqui, DevOps engineer (infrastructure, deployment and monitoring). Omar owns how the platform is hosted, how releases are deployed and how we watch the system for problems. He also manages our day-to-day relationship with our CDN provider, Swiftline Edge. When something is slow or down, Omar is the first engineer to be paged, and he brings in Sam, Jules or Kenji as the problem requires.

Engineering decisions that affect more than one person's area, such as a change to how the editor talks to the viewport, are settled by Arjun.

7. Marketing and design

Ellis Grant, marketing manager (webinars, LinkedIn, trade shows). Ellis owns our outbound marketing: the webinar program, our LinkedIn presence and our trade-show planning. He works with Paloma so that campaigns bring in leads the account executives can use, and with Priya so that announcements match what the product actually does.

Tamsin Hale, product and brand designer. Tamsin owns the look of the product and of the Kitefold brand. That covers editor design, the website, slide templates, and anything customers see that carries our name. Requests for design work go through Priya for product matters and through Ellis for marketing matters, and Tamsin sets the order of work when they compete.

8. Contract 3D artists

We work with two contract 3D artists. Both are independent professionals, not employees. They take requests from Priya and from the customer success team when a client project needs custom 3D content, and they work to the briefs and file standards we give them.

Lena Marshall, contract 3D artist for hard-surface product models. Lena builds clean models of manufactured products, such as packaging, devices and furniture, optimized for the camera experience.

Ivo Petrov, contract 3D artist for photogrammetry scans. Ivo turns real-world objects into scanned 3D models and prepares them so they run well in the browser.

Requests to the contractors should be written down, with the client, the object and the intended plan or use case, so that the work can be scoped before it starts. Sam is the technical contact for questions about file formats and what the viewport can display.

9. Fractional CFO

Bea Kowalczyk of Northbeam Finance is our fractional CFO on a $4,200 monthly retainer. Bea advises on financial planning, reporting and the financial side of major decisions. She works with me directly and is not part of the day-to-day reporting lines above. Questions about budgets, financial reporting or anything involving company finances should come to me first, and I will involve Bea where it is useful.

10. Working across areas

A few simple habits keep the team working well together.

Every customer account has one named owner. For Arena accounts it is Lena Marsh, and for small-business accounts it is Diego Ferreira. Account executives own the deal until signature, and customer success owns the relationship afterward.

Bugs reported by customers enter through Rosa and the Kitefold Care desk. She writes them up, and Arjun or the relevant engineer picks them up. Please do not send customer bugs straight to individual engineers by chat.

Feature requests from customers go to Priya, with the account name and the reason the customer wants it. Priya decides whether it enters the roadmap, and the account owner tells the customer.

Anything we say about the product in public is checked by Priya before Ellis publishes it.

I will update this document whenever someone joins, leaves or changes role. The headcount in section 2 is accurate as of January 19, 2026 and will be restated whenever it changes. If you see something that is out of date, tell me.
