# Kitefold Investor Update: Q4 2025

January 14, 2026

From: Maya Lindqvist, founder and CEO

To: Kitefold investors

Hello all,

This is our update for Q4 2025. MRR rose in each of the three months, product work stayed focused on the editor and on Facecast, and the team ran well. Below you will find the monthly numbers, what went well, what did not, product and hiring notes, and where we would like your help.

Summary

MRR closed the quarter at $68,900 at the end of December 2025, up from $61,000 at the end of October 2025. Growth came from more paying accounts and from existing accounts moving to larger plans. Facecast remains in public beta, and the editor is steadier than it was at the start of the quarter. Our main concern is the pace of enterprise deals, which I cover under lowlights.

MRR by month

End of October 2025: MRR of $61,000.

End of November 2025: MRR of $64,200.

End of December 2025: MRR of $68,900.

The line moved up in each of the three months. The monthly gain was larger in December than in November. We do not read much into a single month, because plan upgrades tend to arrive in clusters. The direction matches what Paloma Reyes and her team see in the pipeline.

Highlights

Steady growth across plan tiers. New business came from agencies and small businesses, and several existing accounts upgraded. Hannah Brook carried most of the agency and small-business pipeline, and Diego Ferreira's work with small-business accounts kept early churn low.

Arena accounts stayed healthy. Wes Okafor continued to work enterprise deals, and Lena Marsh kept our Arena customers closely supported. Their feedback is now a regular input to the product roadmap, and I think that loop is paying off.

A calmer support desk. Rosa Imbert and the Kitefold Care desk kept response quality high through the busy end-of-year campaign period. We will keep staffing support with people, not automation, in front of customers.

Marketing consistency. Ellis Grant kept the webinar, LinkedIn and trade show programs running. Tamsin Hale refreshed the product and brand design so that our materials look more consistent from the website through to the editor.

Engineering delivery. The team shipped steadily without major incidents that reached customers. Sam Achterberg's rendering work, including the Canvas 3D viewport, keeps giving us a strong base for everything built on top of it. Jules Moreau continued improving the editor experience, and Omar Siddiqui kept the delivery infrastructure dependable.

Lowlights

Enterprise cycles are slow. Large deals take longer than we forecast, with security reviews and procurement steps adding weeks to nearly every conversation. We have not lost deals because of this, but the timing is hard to predict, and I would rather say so plainly than let it surprise you later.

Concentration in a few larger accounts. A handful of Arena customers still represent a meaningful part of our MRR. We are working to broaden the base so that no single renewal can move the line much.

Documentation lagged the product. Several features reached customers before their guides were ready. That cost us a few avoidable support conversations, and we are tightening the process so that documentation is part of the definition of done.

Hiring took longer than planned. A few searches took longer than I had hoped. I explain more below.

Product

Facecast stayed in public beta throughout the quarter. We spent most of the time on stability, on feedback from beta users, and on making the experience more forgiving on mid-range phones. Kenji Watanabe leads the tracking work, and his team's priority is consistency across devices and lighting conditions rather than new capabilities. We will leave beta when the numbers say we are ready, not before. We are not giving a date.

In the editor, Priya Kandasamy's team focused on making the first ten minutes easier. New users now reach a published experience with fewer clicks, and fewer of them need help from our support desk. This matters because our whole proposition is that a marketer can build WebAR without writing code.

On the rendering side, we continued to improve how complex product models load and display. Our contract 3D artists, Lena Marshall for hard-surface product models and Ivo Petrov for photogrammetry scans, helped us build a better library of reference models for testing and for sales demos.

Hiring

The team is steady. We have no departures to report. We are looking to add people in a few areas where we are stretched, and we are choosing carefully rather than quickly. Engineering, customer success and sales are the areas under the most pressure. For engineers we use a practical take-home exercise instead of whiteboard puzzles, which gives us a much better read on how people actually work. For sales, we favor candidates who understand how campaigns get bought. If you know someone who fits, please send them our way.

What we are watching

Net revenue retention. We want to see more upgrades than downgrades in every month, and the December gain suggests that is happening.

Time to first published experience for new users. This is our best early signal of whether the editor work is landing.

Enterprise pipeline timing. We are building a clearer picture of the stages and expected durations, so that our forecasts become more reliable.

Facecast beta feedback. We read every report and sort the themes by how often they come up.

Asks

Introductions to enterprise buyers. We are looking for marketing and digital leaders in retail, consumer goods and education who are considering WebAR for the first time. A warm introduction from you shortens our sales cycle more than almost anything else.

Advice on enterprise procurement. If you have seen security reviews and procurement go smoothly or badly at other companies, I would like to hear what made the difference. A twenty-minute call is plenty.

Referrals for hiring. Engineering, customer success and sales candidates in particular. Please forward our open roles to anyone you think would be a fit.

Feedback on this update. If there is something you want to see in the next one that is not here, tell me. I would rather send you what you need than what is easy for me to write.

Closing

Q4 2025 was a quarter of steady progress: three months of rising MRR, a product that is more reliable, and a team that is working well together. The main risks are enterprise timing and concentration, and we are working on both. Thank you for your support and your introductions. Reply to this email or call me any time with questions.

Maya Lindqvist

Founder and CEO, Kitefold, Inc.
