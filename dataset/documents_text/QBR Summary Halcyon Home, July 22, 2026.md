1. Summary

Halcyon Home is one of our Arena accounts, and the review on July 22, 2026 was a healthy one. The catalog keeps growing, shoppers spend real time inside the experience, and the customer came with one clear product request rather than a list of complaints. The request is occlusion in Floorplace. I want product and rendering to read section 4 carefully, because it is the main thing to come out of this review.

2. Account snapshot

Halcyon Home's catalog presence in Floorplace stands at 38 products live in Floorplace (July 22, 2026). The team at Halcyon Home owns the catalog work themselves and publishes through our editor, so growth in that number reflects their own pace rather than a services engagement from us. We discussed how they decide which products go live next, and they described a steady, category-by-category approach rather than a single large push.

Plan fit: the account is on Arena, and nothing in the conversation suggested they feel over- or under-served by the plan. No plan change was raised by either side.

3. Usage and engagement

The headline engagement number from the review is an average session of 71 seconds in the second quarter of 2026. For a camera experience that asks a shopper to point a phone at a room, place furniture and move around it, that is a meaningful amount of attention, and it is the number I would put in front of any prospect in home goods when we have the customer's permission to do so.

A few observations from the discussion:

Shoppers who place a product tend to stay and try it in more than one spot, which is what you would hope for with furniture.

The team at Halcyon Home reads session length as a quality signal, not a vanity metric, and asked us to keep reporting it alongside view counts.

We did not get into conversion or revenue attribution, and I do not want that added to this document or to any follow-up without the customer volunteering it.

4. Customer request: occlusion in Floorplace

Halcyon Home asked for occlusion in Floorplace so sofas can sit behind real coffee tables. At the moment a virtual sofa is drawn on top of everything the camera sees, so a coffee table in the foreground does not hide any part of it. The customer's point was that this breaks the sense of the sofa being in the room, and that it matters most for exactly the products they sell: large pieces placed behind or beside smaller real furniture.

What we said in the room:

We acknowledged the request as a real gap and did not argue the point.

We did not promise a delivery date, a release, or a level of accuracy. The request is logged and the answer on timing is that it is on the roadmap, no date yet.

We said we would come back with an honest read on feasibility from product and rendering, including any limits on which devices and room conditions it could work in.

Internal note for product: the question for Priya Kandasamy and Sam Achterberg is not whether occlusion is desirable, since the customer has made that clear. It is how well we can approximate real objects such as low tables on ordinary phone cameras without making the experience slower or less stable. If a partial solution is possible, for example handling large, flat, low objects first, it would be worth describing that option to Halcyon Home rather than waiting for a complete one.
