# Kitefold Sales Playbook and Discount Approval Policy (February 2026)

Kitefold, Inc. | Port Calloway | Issued February 2, 2026 | Owner: Maya Lindqvist, founder and CEO

1. Purpose

This playbook explains how we sell. It covers the segments we sell to, the questions we ask before we spend time on a deal, how we run a demo, when we offer a trial, who can approve a discount and how much, how we contract Arena, and the 2026 quotas of our two account executives. It is written for the account executives, for Paloma Reyes as Head of Sales, and for anyone in Customer Success who joins a sales conversation. If something here conflicts with a habit you have picked up elsewhere, follow the playbook and tell me so we can fix whichever one is wrong.

We sell a no-code WebAR platform. The people who buy it are rarely technical, and they almost never start by asking about WebAR. They start with a campaign, a product launch, an exhibit or a lesson plan, and they want to know whether they can put a camera experience in front of their audience without hiring a developer. Every part of our sales process is built around answering that question quickly and honestly.

3. Qualification questions

Qualification is not an interrogation. It is a short conversation that tells both sides whether a demo is worth an hour. Ask these in roughly this order, and write the answers into the deal record as you hear them.

a) What are you trying to achieve? Look for a concrete goal: an event, a product launch, a campaign, a course. A vague interest in trying out new technology is a weak signal.

b) Who is the audience, and how will they reach the experience? We are WebAR, so people open a link or scan a code in a browser. Confirm that this suits the audience and the setting.

c) Who decides, and who signs? Find out whether the person you are talking to can approve the spend or needs someone else. For enterprise deals, ask who handles legal and security review.

d) What is the timeline? Work backward from the date the experience must be live. If the date is very close, say plainly what is realistic.

e) What do they use now? Existing tools, an agency, an internal developer, or nothing at all. Do not criticize anything the prospect uses. Ask what has worked and what has not.

f) What does success look like? Ask what number or outcome would make them say the project worked. Write it down in their words.

g) Is there a budget, and is it fixed? You do not need an exact figure, but you need to know whether a range exists and whether it is already approved.

A deal that has clear answers to a, c and d is qualified. A deal that is missing two or more should stay in discovery until it is not. Do not offer a discount to rescue an unqualified deal.

4. Demo flow

A good demo is short, uses the prospect's own material where possible, and ends with a decision about the next step. The flow is the same for every segment, and only the examples change.

Step 1: Restate the goal. Open by repeating, in one or two sentences, what the prospect told you in discovery. This shows you listened and lets them correct you.

Step 2: Build something small, live. Create a simple experience in the editor in front of them. Show how an asset is placed, how the experience is published and how it is opened on a phone. Let them scan or tap it themselves.

Step 3: Show the parts that matter to them. For an agency, that is speed and handoff. For a museum, it is how visitors reach the experience. For enterprise, it is analytics, access control and support. Skip everything else.

Step 4: Talk about delivery, not just creation. Explain how experiences are served and what happens when traffic grows. Keep it plain. Do not promise anything we do not already offer, and if a feature is not available, say that it is on the roadmap with no date yet.

Step 5: Agree on the next step. Before the call ends, name the next action, the owner and the date. If the answer is a trial, move to section 5. If the answer is a quote, state which plan you will quote and which discounts, if any, you expect to apply.

Send a short written recap to everyone on the call after the demo, with the next step and its date. Keep the recap factual and brief.

5. Free trials

Free trials last 14 days and are offered on Kite and Studio only. We do not offer free trials on Stage or Arena. If an enterprise or Stage-level prospect wants to test something before committing, tell them a trial is not available for that plan and offer a longer working session with you or a Customer Success Manager instead.

Before you start a trial, agree on what the prospect will build and what result would make it a success. A trial without a goal usually ends with an unused account. Check in partway through, and book the decision conversation before the 14 days end rather than after.

Do not extend a trial without telling Paloma Reyes. A trial that has to be extended usually means the goal was never clear, and we should fix that rather than add days.

6. Discount approval levels

List price is the starting point for every quote. Discounts are the exception, not the opening offer, and we only give one when we get something in return, such as a longer term, a clear decision date or a reference. The approval levels are as follows.

Up to 10% off list: account executives may give up to 10% off list on their own authority. Record the discount and the reason in the deal record.

Above 10% off list: anything above 10% needs the CEO's approval. Send the request to me with the deal, the segment, the plan, the reason for the request and what we receive in return. Do not tell the prospect that a larger discount is likely until I have answered.

The segment discounts in section 7 and the multi-year discounts in section 8 are standing policy, so you do not need a separate approval to apply one of them to a qualifying deal. Any other combination of discounts, or any extra discount on top of them, is a request to me before you quote it.

Never promise a discount verbally before it is approved. If a prospect asks on a call, say you will confirm and come back to them in writing.

7. Segment discounts

Two segments receive standing discounts off list price:

Education: 40% off list.

Nonprofits and museums: 30% off list.

Confirm eligibility before you quote. For education, check that the buyer is a school, college, university or similar institution. For nonprofits and museums, ask for a simple statement of status, such as the organization's registered nonprofit standing or its museum status. An agency buying for a qualifying organization should tell you whose name will be on the order, and the discount follows the organization that uses the plan. If eligibility is unclear, ask Paloma Reyes before you quote.

8. Multi-year discounts

We offer a discount for customers who commit to a longer term:

2-year term: 8% off.

3-year term: 12% off.

In both cases the term is paid annually, meaning the customer is invoiced once per year and not the full amount up front. Make this clear in the quote so there is no surprise about when payments are due. Multi-year terms are a good trade because they give the customer a stable price and give us a longer relationship. Offer them when the prospect is clearly planning to keep using the platform, and do not use them to cover an unclear fit.

9. How Arena is contracted

Every Arena deal needs a signed order form. Arena is never sold by card checkout, and you should not send an Arena prospect to a self-serve checkout page under any circumstances, even to make a small start.

The reason is simple: Arena customers have security, legal and procurement requirements that a card payment cannot satisfy, and they expect a written agreement that names the plan, the term and the commercial terms. Wes Okafor owns the order form process for Arena. Prepare the order form with the final plan, term and any approved discount, route it for any required approval, and send it for signature. Do not start provisioning an Arena account until the signed order form is in the deal record. When the order form is signed, hand the account over to Lena Marsh with a short note on what was promised and who the key contacts are.

Quotas are set in new ARR, meaning annual recurring revenue from new customers. Renewals and expansion are tracked separately and do not count toward these numbers.

Wes Okafor, Account Executive for enterprise (Arena) deals: 2026 quota of $420,000 in new ARR.

These quotas apply to the 2026 calendar year. Paloma Reyes reviews pipeline against them with each account executive regularly, and I join when a deal needs my approval. Discounts reduce the ARR a deal contributes, so a discount that wins a deal still has to leave the deal worth winning.

11. Keeping the deal record honest

Every deal record should show the segment, the plan being quoted, the answers to the qualification questions, the next step with its date, and any discount with its reason and approver. Move a deal back a stage when the evidence says it has gone backward. A pipeline that is accurate and smaller is more useful to all of us than one that is large and wrong.

Notes from calls go in the record on the same day, while the details are still clear. When a deal is lost, record the reason in plain words. We read these reasons together and use them to improve this playbook.
