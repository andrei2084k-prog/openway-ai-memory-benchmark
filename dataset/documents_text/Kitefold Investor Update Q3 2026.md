# Kitefold Investor Update: Q3 2026

October 12, 2026

To our investors and advisors,

This is our update for the third quarter of 2026. The short version: MRR closed September at $97,900, we ended the quarter with 517 paying accounts, and we have 28 months of runway. It was also a busy quarter operationally. We finished the move to a new CDN provider, retired the Classic editor, shipped the first beta of Floorplace occlusion, and changed who leads sales and customer success. Details follow.

Headline numbers

Paying accounts: 517 paying accounts on September 30, 2026.

MRR: $91,700 at the end of July 2026, $90,400 at the end of August 2026 and $97,900 at the end of September 2026.

Net burn: $214,000 a month in the third quarter of 2026.

Runway: 28 months of runway at September 30, 2026.

Support: median first response of 3 hours 10 minutes on the Kitefold Care desk in the third quarter of 2026.

Kitefold for Shopify: 140 installs by September 30, 2026.

Revenue and accounts

The MRR path through the quarter was not a straight line, and we would rather say so plainly. We went from $91,700 at the end of July to $90,400 at the end of August, a step backward, and then to $97,900 at the end of September. We do not read much into any single month. Our revenue is subscription-based, and the timing of renewals, plan changes and cancellations can move a month in either direction. What we watch is the pattern across the whole quarter, and September closed well above where July started.

The 517 paying accounts span all of our plans, from Kite through Arena. We do not break out account counts by plan in investor updates. The mix is moving in the direction we want, with more agencies and brands running several live projects on the same account, and with Arena accounts such as Tallgrass & Pike, Juniper & Rye, Okapi Outdoor and Halcyon Home continuing to anchor our larger deployments. Education and cultural institutions remain a steady part of the base. We also had two cancellations among named accounts that we followed closely, and we use them as input when we review onboarding and renewal practice.

Cash and runway

Net burn averaged $214,000 a month across the third quarter of 2026. At September 30, 2026 that left us with 28 months of runway. We kept spending disciplined during the quarter. The main additions were hires in sales, customer success and solutions engineering, which we made deliberately and which are already built into the burn figure above. Moving off our previous CDN provider also removed a vendor relationship we no longer needed. We have no plans to change our spending posture in the near term, and we will tell you before we do.

Product

Four product items matter most from the quarter.

First, the Classic editor was retired on September 30, 2026. We had moved the retirement date up, and we did so because maintaining two editors was slowing down everything else the product team wanted to do. Priya Kandasamy, our Head of Product, and her team put considerable effort into migration guidance, and Jules Moreau and the editor team made sure Canvas covered the workflows that Classic users depended on. Customers now work in one editor, which simplifies support, documentation and training.

Second, Legacy Marker tracking was removed on July 1, 2026, as scheduled after its deprecation in release 4.0 on March 3, 2026. The 37 projects that were still using it were moved to Image Anchor automatically. We expected some friction from a removal like this and prepared for it, and the migration generated very little follow-up.

Third, release 4.5 on August 25, 2026 brought Floorplace occlusion in beta, available on Stage and Arena only. Occlusion lets virtual objects sit convincingly behind real ones, which has been one of the most common requests from retail and exhibit customers. It is a beta, and we are collecting feedback from the accounts using it before we widen availability. Sam Achterberg, our senior engineer for rendering, leads that work. Separately, Facecast has been generally available since release 4.2 on May 19, 2026 and tracks up to 3 faces at once. Adoption among agencies has been steady through the quarter.

Fourth, Kitefold for Shopify, which launched on June 10, 2026, reached 140 installs by September 30, 2026. That is an encouraging first full quarter for a new distribution channel. We are not reporting revenue or conversion figures for the app yet, because we want a longer baseline before we draw conclusions. For now we treat installs as a measure of interest, not of business impact.

Infrastructure

On August 17, 2026 all traffic moved from our previous CDN to Petrel CDN, completing the migration. Omar Siddiqui, our DevOps engineer, led the project from planning through cutover, and our contract with the previous provider ended on the same date. The migration was motivated in part by the outage of April 16, 2026, which lasted 3 hours 40 minutes, from 09:10 to 12:50 Pacific. That incident was painful for customers and for us, and we committed to reducing our dependence on a single point of failure. Corin Vale and the team at Petrel were responsive partners through the cutover. We will not publish uptime figures in this update, but we have kept the operational runbooks current and are reviewing them regularly.

Customer support

Rosa Imbert and the Kitefold Care desk posted a median first response of 3 hours 10 minutes in the third quarter of 2026. We think this is a good number for a team of our size, and it was achieved while the Classic retirement and the CDN move were both generating questions. Our approach to support remains simple: a person answers. We have been improving internal documentation so that answers are consistent no matter who picks up the conversation.

Wes Okafor continues to cover enterprise Arena deals and Hannah Brook covers agencies and small business, and both now report into Marcus. We also said goodbye to two colleagues. Paloma Reyes left on June 19, 2026 to join a larger software company, and Kenji Watanabe left on August 28, 2026 to start his own studio. We are grateful for their work and wish them well. Their responsibilities have been absorbed by the existing team while we assess whether to backfill.

Partnerships and marketing

Noor Haddad, who has been our partnerships manager since March 9, 2026, continued building relationships with agencies and platform partners. Ellis Grant ran our webinar, LinkedIn and trade show programs. Tamsin Hale supported the quarter with product and brand design, including materials for the Classic retirement and the Floorplace beta. We are still learning which channels produce the best-fit accounts and we are paying close attention to quality over volume.

Risks and watch items

We want to be candid about what we are watching.

1. MRR volatility. The August dip shows that a single month can mislead. We will keep reporting monthly figures so you can see the pattern for yourselves.

2. Leadership transitions. We changed the heads of sales and customer success within two months of each other. Both are strong additions, but new leaders need time to settle into their roles and to build their own rhythms with the team.

3. Beta quality. Floorplace occlusion is new and complex. We would rather hold the beta longer than widen it before it is ready.

4. Concentration in larger accounts. Our Arena accounts matter a great deal to revenue, and we are working with Diego and Lena to make sure each one has a clear success plan.

Where we could use your help

Introductions to agencies that build campaigns for consumer brands remain the most valuable help you can offer. We would also welcome introductions to retail and e-commerce operators who might be interested in the Shopify app. If you know a founder or operator who has run a similar leadership transition, we would be glad to hear what worked for them.

Closing

The third quarter ended with higher MRR than it began with, a longer list of accounts, a cleaner product, a new infrastructure foundation and a stronger leadership bench. It also included a soft August that we are not hiding. With 28 months of runway, we have room to be patient and to keep investing in quality. Thank you, as always, for your support and your candor. Please reply with any questions and I will answer each one personally.

Maya Lindqvist
